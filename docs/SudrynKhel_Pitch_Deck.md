# ANGLE 1 — The 300-Word Crisis

> Today, seven Mongolian daily newspapers combined use only **2,944 distinct words**. State documents cycle through **300**. This is a language collapse in progress.

## The crisis in numbers

| Metric | Count |
|--------|-------|
| Words in state documents | ~300 |
| Words across 7 daily newspapers | 2,944 |
| Pages in the 108 Ganjuur volumes | 41,588 |

Modern Mongolian has become extremely shallow on philosophical, religious, and expressive vocabulary. Words for "compassion," "impermanence," "merit," "emptiness," "discernment" are disappearing — not because they are outdated, but because **no one made them findable**.

## The asset

The 108 volumes of the Ganjuur — carved into stone woodblocks in **1717–1720** under Manchu Emperor Enkh-Amgalan — are the single deepest, most logically structured Mongolian ever written. **41,588 pages.** Hundreds of thousands of words of philosophical, ethical, and meditative instruction. Today they sit in archives, private collections, and monasteries without a canonical digital form.

## What we do

We unlock them: digitize, classify, transliterate, link them to their source languages, and feed the vocabulary back into Mongolian life. We make a dead library live.

# ANGLE 2 — The Rosetta Corpus

> The same Buddhist canon was recorded in seven languages across two millennia. Right now those versions cannot speak to each other.

## The opportunity

| Language | Role |
|----------|------|
| Tibetan | Original Buddhist recitation |
| Sanskrit | Philosophical root terms |
| Mongolian | Working sanctuary version |
| Manchu | Imperial court version |
| Classical Chinese | Medical/philosophical parallel |
| Old Uyghur | Turkic bridge language |
| English · French | Western scholarly tradition |

A Ulaanbaatar scholar reading only Mongolian is locked out of 90% of Buddhist scholarship. A Tibetan monk cannot trace how a Sanskrit concept mutated across the Silk Road into Mongolian. These are the same ideas — separated by alphabet.

## The product

One index. Seven living languages. One Mongolian spine. Every word in the 108 Ganjuur volumes, linked to its **Sanskrit etymological root**, its **Tibetan canonical parallel**, its **Manchu court variant**, and its **English/French scholarly translation**.

This is not a national project. This is **cross-cultural infrastructure** with UNESCO-level gravitational pull.
