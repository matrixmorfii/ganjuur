# СУДРЫН ХЭЛ · Presentation Script & Speaker Notes
## Mongolian Language Preservation through the Ganjuur Canon

---

# SLIDE 1 — TITLE + OPENING

**Speaker:**
"Today, seven Mongolian newspapers combined publish just **2,944 distinct words** in a day. A state document cycles through roughly **300**. That is not normal. That is a language collapse in progress — and we have the cure."

**Visual:** Two large numbers on screen:
- `2,944` (Mongolian press vocabulary)
- `41,588` (Ganjuur pages)

---

# SLIDE 2 — THE CRISIS

**Speaker:**
"A full national conversation runs on fewer words than a typical English grocery list. Words like compassion, impermanence, merit, emptiness, discernment — the words that built Buddhist civilization in this country for 800 years — are not gone from the language, but they are gone from daily life. Nobody reads them now because nobody can find them."

**Visual:** Word-cloud shrinking — philosophical terms fading to grey.

---

# SLIDE 3 — THE ASSET

**Speaker:**
"This is the Ganjuur. 108 volumes. Carved into stone woodblocks between 1717 and 1720 by order of the Manchu emperor. 41,588 pages of the deepest, most logically structured Mongolian ever written. It is the answer to the collapse — it is sitting in archives, unexploited."

**Visual:** Photo or rendering of шунхан бар (woodblock) printing.

---

# SLIDE 4 — THE PRODUCT MAP

**Speaker:**
"We don't just scan old books. We produce five things.
First — the canonical digital edition, Cyrillic and traditional.
Second — a thematic search and categorization platform.
Third — a thesaurus that links every word to its Sanskrit, Tibetan, and Old Uyghur roots.
Fourth — a free, online dictionary called **Судрын хэлний үгийн сан** open to every speaker.
Fifth — a 30-credit university program to carry the next generation back into the old script."

**Visual:** Five-product table with icons.

# SLIDE 5 — THE ROSSETA BRIDGE ANGLE

**Speaker:**
"The Ganjuur doesn't live alone. The same canon exists in Tibetan, Sanskrit, Manchu, Chinese, Old Uyghur, and later English and French. These are the same ideas, but separated by alphabet. A Mongolian scholar cannot currently bridge across. We are building the first unified index — one spine, seven languages. This is a global cultural infrastructure play."

**Visual:** Central Mongolian node, seven surrounding language nodes, linking lines.
